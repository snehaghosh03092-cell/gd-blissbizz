# Starting a small business in India: the BlissBiz starter guide

Reviewed on 2026-11-19 against the sources listed under each section, prioritising current Government of India portals. This is general information for first-time founders, **not legal, tax or financial advice**. Rules and limits change; always confirm on the official portal named in each section (or with a chartered accountant) before acting. The BlissBiz assistant answers new founders **only** from this guide.

Format (read by the assistant): every section starts with `## [id] Title`, then a `keywords:` line, then bullet points, then `sources:` lines (`- Title | URL`).

## [start-steps] How to start, step by step
keywords: start, begin, starting, steps, step, how, process, roadmap, plan, first, launch, new, business, optimised, optimized, smart, way, idea, guide
- 1. Pick an idea that matches your skills and what people near you already buy (see "Business ideas for a small budget").
- 2. Test demand cheaply first: sell to 10-20 friends, neighbours or a WhatsApp group before buying lots of stock.
- 3. Make a simple budget: one-time setup costs, first stock, monthly costs, and keep a few months of running costs as a buffer.
- 4. Choose a structure: most first businesses start as a sole proprietorship (cheapest, simplest); see "Choosing a business structure".
- 5. Get the basic papers: PAN, a bank account for the business, Udyam registration (free), and only the licences your activity needs (see "Registrations and papers").
- 6. Set up your online shop here in BlissBiz: business details, stock location, look, policies, products, then Go live.
- 7. Price for profit, not just to be cheap (see "Pricing for profit"), and track every sale and expense from day one.
- 8. Grow step by step: reinvest profits, add products that sell, drop what doesn't (see "Growing sales").

## [ideas-budget] Business ideas for a small budget (around ₹1 lakh)
keywords: idea, ideas, what, start, capital, budget, lakh, money, invest, investment, small, low, cheap, home, side, business, suggest, options, 1, 100000, 50000, rupees, don't, dont, know, no
- Freelance services (writing, design, coding, video editing, social media management): mostly needs a laptop and internet; find clients on platforms or locally.
- Home bakery, tiffin or catering: low equipment cost; needs FSSAI registration (see "Food businesses").
- Tutoring or coaching (school subjects, music, yoga, fitness classes): skill-based, very low setup cost.
- Handmade products (soaps, candles, crafts, jewellery): small batches, sell online and at local markets.
- Custom printing (t-shirts, mugs, gifts): small machine or print-on-demand suppliers.
- Phone accessories, stationery or small online store: buy small quantities from wholesalers, sell online.
- Pet care (walking, grooming, boarding), digital marketing for local shops: service businesses with little stock.
- How to choose: pick what you already know + what sells near you. Spend first on testing demand and a little stock, not on a big setup. Keep part of the budget as a buffer for running costs.
- Costs vary a lot by city and scale; these are starting points from published lists, not guarantees of profit.

## [structure] Choosing a business structure
keywords: structure, proprietorship, proprietor, sole, partnership, llp, private, limited, company, pvt, opc, one, person, register, which, choose, type, entity, liability
- Sole proprietorship: cheapest and simplest, minimal compliance, but the owner is personally liable for business debts. Good for freelancers, small traders and most first businesses.
- Partnership / LLP: for two or more founders. An LLP limits each partner's liability; it costs more to set up and file than a proprietorship (audit needed only above certain turnover or contribution levels).
- One Person Company (OPC): a solo founder who wants limited liability, with more compliance than a proprietorship.
- Private limited company: the most compliance and cost (annual audit, board meetings, MCA filings) but best if you plan to raise investment or serve large corporate clients. Incorporated through the SPICe+ form, which also gives PAN and TAN.
- Rule of thumb: solo and small, start as a proprietor; partners, consider an LLP; raising money, a private limited company.

- Startup India: Legal considerations and entity choices | https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/Legal-Considerations-for-a-Startup-.html

## [registrations] Registrations and papers you need
keywords: papers, paper, documents, document, registration, registrations, register, licence, license, licences, licenses, legal, legally, compliance, checklist, need, required, permission, permit, approval, pan, tan, bank, account, current, trademark, shop, establishment, trade, professional, tax, epf, esi, startup, india, dpiit, regulatory
- PAN: needed for the business (a proprietor uses their own PAN; companies and LLPs get one at incorporation). Companies also get TAN to deduct tax at source.
- Bank account: open a separate current account for the business; keep personal and business money apart.
- Udyam (MSME) registration: free, online, Aadhaar-based; gives access to MSME schemes and loans (see "Udyam registration").
- GST registration: only when you cross the turnover threshold or fall in a mandatory case (see "GST").
- Shops and Establishments registration: a state-level labour registration for shops and offices, usually within about 30 days of starting (some states allow longer); rules and fees differ by state.
- Trade licence: from your municipal corporation for many shops and trades; check with your local municipality.
- Food businesses: FSSAI registration or licence (see "Food businesses").
- Professional tax: a state tax in some states, paid by the business and deducted from salaries.
- With employees: EPF typically applies from 20 employees and ESI from 10 employees (for lower-paid staff); check current limits.
- Brand name: search the trademark registry (IP India) before you fix a name, and consider registering your name or logo.
- Startup India (DPIIT) recognition: optional and meant for eligible innovative or scalable entities; check the current entity, age and turnover criteria on the official recognition page before applying.

## [gst] GST: when you need it and what it costs
keywords: gst, gstin, goods, services, tax, registration, threshold, limit, turnover, 40, 20, lakh, composition, scheme, inter, state, interstate, ecommerce, e-commerce, online, amazon, flipkart, marketplace, invoice
- Threshold: in most states, GST registration is required once yearly turnover crosses ₹40 lakh for goods or ₹20 lakh for services (lower limits, ₹20 lakh / ₹10 lakh, in some special-category states). Mixed goods + services follow the lower limit.
- Mandatory regardless of turnover: making inter-state supplies of goods and a few other cases. Check the GST portal for your case.
- Selling through e-commerce marketplaces: since 1 October 2023, small sellers below the threshold can sell goods through an e-commerce operator without GST registration, but only within one state (no inter-state sales) and with a PAN and an enrolment number on the GST portal.
- Composition scheme: small businesses (up to ₹1.5 crore turnover for traders, manufacturers and restaurants without alcohol; ₹50 lakh for service providers) can pay a low fixed rate on turnover (for example 1% for traders, 5% for such restaurants) with simpler returns, but cannot charge GST on invoices, cannot claim input credit and cannot make inter-state sales.
- In BlissBiz: if you are GST-registered, enter your GSTIN in Shop setup and your invoices will be tax invoices; otherwise they are bills of supply without GST.
- Limits and lists of special-category states differ between sources; confirm on the GST portal or CBIC before relying on a number.

- CBIC: CGST Act registration provisions | https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/CGST-Act-2017-amended-01012022.pdf

## [udyam] Udyam (MSME) registration
keywords: udyam, msme, micro, small, medium, enterprise, udyog, aadhaar, certificate, free, registration, benefits, scheme, schemes
- Udyam registration is free, fully online, paperless and based on self-declaration; it needs Aadhaar (plus PAN, and GSTIN where applicable). No fee is to be paid to anyone.
- There is no renewal; investment and turnover details are picked up from government databases using PAN and GSTIN.
- From 1 April 2025, a micro enterprise is one with investment up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover up to ₹10 crore (small: up to ₹25 crore / ₹100 crore).
- Benefits include access to MSME schemes and priority-sector loans.
- Apply only on the official portal udyamregistration.gov.in.
sources:
- Udyam Registration portal (official) | https://udyamregistration.gov.in/Important.aspx

## [food] Food businesses (home bakery, cakes, tiffin, catering)
keywords: food, fssai, bakery, bake, cake, cakes, tiffin, catering, kitchen, home, snacks, sweets, restaurant, cafe, licence, license, foscos
- Every food business needs the applicable FSSAI registration or licence before selling, including home kitchens, home bakers and tiffin services.
- From 1 April 2026, the general turnover categories are Registration up to ₹1.5 crore, State Licence above ₹1.5 crore and up to ₹50 crore, and Central Licence above ₹50 crore.
- FSSAI registrations and licences now have perpetual validity unless suspended, cancelled or surrendered; the operator must still meet continuing food-safety requirements.
- Product type, production capacity and other eligibility rules can require a different category even when turnover is low. Use FoSCoS “Eligibility of your food business” before applying.
- Apply and pay only through the official FoSCoS portal.
sources:
- FSSAI: 2026 licensing and registration amendment FAQs | https://fssai.gov.in/docs/whatsnew/advisories-orders/69c6a23234827order_27032026.pdf
- FSSAI: official FoSCoS portal | https://foscos.fssai.gov.in/

## [funding] Loans and funding for a small start
keywords: loan, loans, funding, fund, finance, money, capital, borrow, mudra, shishu, kishore, kishor, tarun, bank, investor, investment, raise, credit, scheme, government
- Compare MSME and bank loan options only after you know the smallest amount the business actually needs; loan eligibility, interest and available categories can change.
- Udyam registration can help an eligible enterprise access MSME schemes and priority-sector lending.
- Start with your own capital where possible, borrow only for things that earn money (stock that sells, equipment you use daily), and keep repayments well below expected monthly profit.
- For MUDRA or another scheme, confirm the current category, limit, security and repayment terms with an authorised lender before applying.

- Udyam Registration portal: official MSME guidance | https://udyamregistration.gov.in/Important.aspx

## [pricing-profit] Pricing for profit
keywords: price, pricing, prices, profit, profits, profitable, margin, margins, markup, cost, costs, break, even, breakeven, earn, earning, money, discount, discounts, loss, make
- Count your full cost per item: materials or purchase price, packaging, delivery, platform or payment fees.
- Markup is not margin: an item costing ₹100 sold at ₹150 is a 50% markup but only a 33% margin.
- To get a target margin: selling price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin). Example: cost ₹700, 30% margin, so the price is ₹1,000 (not ₹910).
- Break-even: units to sell each month = monthly fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit). Example: ₹100 price, ₹60 variable cost, so you earn ₹40 per unit towards fixed costs.
- Check discounts at the discounted price: a 20% discount can wipe out most of a thin margin.
- In BlissBiz: the Insights page has a discount what-if that shows the new margin and how many more units you would need to sell to earn the same profit.

## [grow-sales] Growing sales and profit
keywords: grow, growth, increase, more, sales, sell, customers, marketing, promote, promotion, advertise, social, instagram, whatsapp, repeat, profits, scale, improve, boost, demand
- Sell first to people who already trust you (friends, neighbours, local groups), then ask for reviews and referrals.
- Show your products where your customers are: WhatsApp catalogue, Instagram, local markets, and your BlissBiz shop link.
- Keep best sellers always in stock; drop or discount what doesn't sell (BlissBiz Insights flags both).
- Repeat customers are cheaper than new ones: follow up, offer small loyalty perks, deliver on time.
- Track every rupee: weekly sales, costs, and profit per product; reinvest profit into what sells.
- Grow in small steps: test a new product in small quantities before buying a lot.
- These are general small-business practices (BlissBiz guidance), not figures from a specific source.
